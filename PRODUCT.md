# Product

## Register

product

## Users

High school through college students in two tracks:

**Voice Acting** — aspiring voice actors, students, and hobbyists practicing commercial, audiobook, character/animation, or narration work. Using the app between lessons and self-study sessions to practice against real scripts and receive feedback that feels like a coach, not a rubric.

**Theatre** — student performers, community theatre participants, drama school students. Same context: practicing independently, building confidence between rehearsals.

Both groups are at entry-to-intermediate experience. They've chosen this because they care about their craft. The primary job to be done: record a take, receive specific feedback anchored to real moments in the performance, and know exactly what to work on next.

## Product Purpose

Voxara is a coaching platform for voice acting and theatre performance practice. Users select from curated scenarios, record audio (voice acting) or video (theatre), and receive AI-generated feedback structured like a real coaching session — not a score sheet. Feedback references specific transcript moments and timestamps, differentiates by the performer's subtype and goals, and suggests concrete adjustments rather than generic observations.

Success looks like: a student finishes a session and feels they heard something specific and actionable from someone who understood what they were going for — not like they were graded by an algorithm.

## Brand Personality

Encouraging, specific, non-judgmental.

The voice of a skilled coach who understands that performance is subjective art. This product expands what a performer can do — it never flattens their work into "right" or "wrong." It notices what's working. It names exactly where growth is happening and what to try next. It treats the user as a developing artist, not a student being tested.

## Anti-references

- Enterprise SaaS: blue dashboards, hero metric cards, feature grids, corporate tone. Too impersonal and institutional for a performing arts context.
- Gamified apps (Duolingo, streaks, XP, confetti): trivializes the craft; the target user is serious about improving, not collecting badges.
- Gothic / dark aesthetic: the brief explicitly excludes this. The mood is focused and creative, not moody.
- Blue and purple accents: too generic and tech-default; the palette should feel creatively distinct.
- Clinical / form-heavy: white sterile layouts read as healthcare or bureaucracy, not a place where artistic work happens.

## Design Principles

1. **Coach, not judge** — every feedback surface frames growth as expansive, not evaluative. Scores exist to orient, not rank. Growth areas come with a path forward.
2. **Performance is human** — the interface is a creative space. It should feel like a studio antechamber: focused, purposeful, not software-shaped. The user is about to do something that requires presence.
3. **Specificity over generics** — feedback is only valuable when it names a moment. The UI structure should reinforce this: timestamps, quoted phrases, concrete suggestions surface directly, not buried.
4. **Focus over chrome** — a student preparing a take doesn't need distractions. Navigation, decorative elements, and UI chrome are subordinate to the task at hand. Every screen has one primary job.
5. **Earned clarity** — structure and hierarchy serve the performer's need to quickly find, parse, and act on feedback. Dense information is fine; confusing information is not.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG level committed yet. Good-faith target: readable contrast, keyboard-navigable flows, reduced-motion support for animations. Avoid motion or visual effects that would block users with vestibular sensitivity. Screen reader–friendly form labels throughout onboarding and feedback screens.
